A personal project by Mazin Kanuga

I wanted a number
I could check.

My money was already in Lunch Money. My zakat calculation should start there, too.

The problem was the guessing.

Online calculators ask you how much is in your accounts, how much you owe, and what else you own. The arithmetic is straightforward. Getting the account totals right is the part that takes the work.

I built Mizan to start with the balances I already track in Lunch Money. It reads the accounts, lets me review what belongs in the calculation, and shows the path from those amounts to the zakat estimate.

Every dollar should have a trail.

An imported balance is marked as imported. If I change it here, it is marked as adjusted. Cash at home, gold, silver and anything else I add are kept visible as additions. The final total can be opened up, line by line.

The graphs help explain the same numbers. They show how the account balances become a total, what changes with a different method, and what the available monthly history looks like.

The numbers come from my budget.
The method is still my choice.

Lunch Money can supply account balances. It cannot decide which zakat rules I follow, value things I have not recorded, or make an outdated bank connection current. Mizan makes those choices and data gaps visible so I can review them.

That is the point of this project: less account-entry guesswork, precise arithmetic, and a calculation I can understand.

You can explore more tools on Lunch Money's developer page, or read the official API documentation to see what the integration is built on.
